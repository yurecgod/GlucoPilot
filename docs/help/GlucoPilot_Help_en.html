What GlucoPilot AI is

GlucoPilot AI is a research diary for adults (18+) with diabetes who use a CGM through xDrip+. It collects glucose, insulin, food and activity, builds an offline forecast and looks for personal patterns in your history.

GlucoPilot is not a medical device. Forecasts, ISF, ICR and “equivalents” are research estimates, not dosing recommendations. Make treatment decisions together with your doctor.

What the app does NOT do

  • It does not raise low or high glucose alarms. Use xDrip+ or your sensor app for alarms.
  • It does not calculate insulin doses.
  • It does not use generative AI and does not send your data to a cloud.

What you need

  • An Android phone with xDrip+ that already receives sensor readings.
  • Internet only for product search in Open Food Facts. Everything else works offline.

First start and language

  1. Open GlucoPilot. There are four tabs at the top: Overview, Diary, Tools, Lab.
  2. Tap ⚙ Settings and choose a language: Українська, English, Español, Deutsch or Français. The app restarts in that language.
  3. Help always opens in the interface language. There is no separate switch.
  4. Connect xDrip+ (see “Connecting xDrip+”). The first reading appears after the next sensor measurement.
  5. Log food and insulin. The more complete the diary, the more useful the forecast and analytics become.

No account is needed. All data stays on this phone.

Connecting xDrip+

GlucoPilot receives glucose only from xDrip+, through the local Android broadcast com.eveningoutpost.dexdrip.BgEstimate. If xDrip+ gets its data from another app (for example Juggluco), leave that chain unchanged.

Glucose (required)

  1. In xDrip+ open Settings → Inter-app settings.
  2. Turn on Broadcast locally.
  3. Leave Identify receiver empty or enter ua.glucopilot.ai.historyqa.multilang.
  4. In GlucoPilot open Tools and make sure Receive CGM broadcasts is checked.
  5. Allow notifications. The background service shows a permanent notification — this is how Android allows data to be received while the screen is off.

Insulin from xDrip+ (optional)

  1. In xDrip+ Inter-app settings, turn on xDrip Web Service.
  2. In GlucoPilot (Tools) turn on Import insulin from xDrip+.
  3. While GlucoPilot is open, it reads insulin records from xDrip+ on the same phone once a minute (address 127.0.0.1, no internet).
Imported boluses are saved as your rapid insulin from ⚙ Settings → My rapid insulin (NovoRapid by default). Basal records from xDrip+ are skipped — enter Tresiba manually in the Diary.

Checking

Below the switch you can see when the last record arrived. Lock the phone for 10–15 minutes and check that the time has updated.

Battery saving

Xiaomi/Redmi (HyperOS, MIUI), Samsung, Huawei and others may stop background work. For both GlucoPilot and xDrip+:

  • set battery usage to Unrestricted;
  • on Xiaomi, enable Autostart;
  • do not swipe the apps away from the recent apps list. A force stop turns off background reception until the app is opened again.

Overview

The Overview tab shows the current state and the main reports. New CGM readings update the page without jumping back to the top.

Glucose now

  • The large number is the latest xDrip+ reading. If it is older than 12 minutes, No recent readings is shown: an outdated reading must not be treated as current.
  • The change since the previous CGM reading and a short data-quality check for the last hour (freshness and gaps, not sensor accuracy).

Forecast

  • Forecast horizons: +15, +30, +45, +60, +90, +120 and +180 minutes with an uncertainty range.
  • Forecast vs actual CGM: how an earlier forecast turned out. Modes: Completed, Current, History. Details in “Forecast” and “Forecast checking”.

Other cards

  • ISF/ICR table — personal research coefficients by time of day (see “ISF and ICR”).
  • Food and NovoRapid · 24 hours — meals, related boluses and the CGM change afterwards. Matching records are not an ICR.
  • Metrics overview — mean glucose and variability over 7 days, a 24-hour chart (green zone 3.9–10.0 mmol/L), GMI and your lab HbA1c log.
  • Offline CGM analysis — the current situation and a search for repeating patterns (see “CGM analytics”).

Detailed technical reports open with the ▼ buttons.

Forecast

The forecast is calculated on the phone, without internet and without a language model. It updates after a new CGM reading or a new diary entry.

When a forecast is available

  • The latest CGM reading is no older than 12 minutes.
  • There are at least 4 readings in the last 30 minutes with no gaps longer than 12 minutes.

How it is built

  1. Trend. The glucose slope over the last 30 minutes. Its effect fades gradually, so far horizons do not extend the line indefinitely.
  2. Known events. The change in insulin on board (IOB) and carbohydrates still being absorbed (COB) between now and the horizon.
  3. Personal calibration. The effect of IOB and COB is estimated from your last 30 days (hourly windows, outcome after 60 minutes). It is switched on only when there are at least 8 usable windows and the model beats the simple trend on the most recent held-out windows. Otherwise the forecast relies on the trend alone.
  4. Range. It widens with the horizon, fast glucose changes, sensor noise and unexplained events.

Limitations

  • The model does not know about future meals, injections, exercise, stress or illness.
  • Missing or inaccurate entries make the forecast worse.
  • Sensor lag, calibrations and CGM errors carry over into the forecast.
Do not use the forecast to calculate a dose or instead of CGM alarms.

Forecast checking

GlucoPilot checks its own forecasts honestly: each forecast is saved first and then compared with the following real CGM readings.

  • One forecast is saved per CGM reading, and only when the CGM is fresh (no older than 10 minutes). Forecasts are also saved in the background, at most every 15 minutes, even when the app is closed.
  • A +30-minute forecast is matched with the first reading taken no earlier than 30 minutes later. Earlier readings do not count.
  • The log keeps up to 1,500 forecast points over 30 days.

Figures in “Accuracy verification”

  • MAE — mean absolute difference between forecast and measurement, mmol/L.
  • Within range — share of measurements that fell inside the forecast range.
  • Median and P90 — typical and large (90th percentile) error.
  • Bias — systematic overestimation (+) or underestimation (−).
  • 3-hour blocks — an approximate number of independent periods. While there are few, conclusions are preliminary.

If you logged new food or insulin after a forecast, this is marked: the old forecast is not changed, but its error was made under different conditions.

Diary

The Diary records events that affect glucose. Choose the type of the new event.

  • Insulin — dose in units and the product name. For Tresiba, tick Basal insulin.
  • Food — carbohydrates are required; protein and fat are optional. An empty field means “unknown”, not zero. Calories are calculated automatically (4/4/9 kcal per gram of carbs, protein and fat) when protein and fat are entered.
  • Activity — type, duration and optionally average heart rate and power.
  • Glucose — a manual meter measurement.

Insulin names

Only recognised names count as rapid insulin: rapid-acting analogs (NovoRapid, NovoLog, Humalog, Apidra, aspart, lispro, glulisine), ultra-rapid (Fiasp, Lyumjev) and regular human insulin (Actrapid, Humulin R, Insuman Rapid). Basal: Tresiba, Lantus, Levemir, Toujeo, Basaglar, degludec, glargine, detemir. An entry with an unknown name is saved but not counted in insulin on board. The product for new entries and for xDrip+ import is set in ⚙ Settings → My rapid insulin.

History

  • Injections and food entries can be edited and deleted.
  • A meal can be saved to My Meals and added again with one tap.
  • Today's meals totals carbohydrates, protein, fat and calories.

Diary changes recalculate analytics and insulin on board, but forecasts that were already saved are not changed.

Food catalog (Open Food Facts)

The food catalog searches packaged products in the open Open Food Facts database.

  1. In the Diary tap Food catalog · nutrition.
  2. Enter a name (at least 2 characters) or a barcode and tap Search. The search language matches the interface language.
  3. Choose a product, set the portion in grams and add it to the meal. You can combine several products.
  4. Check the values and tap Confirm and save.

Important

  • The data is entered by community members. Compare it with the label.
  • Fill in missing values manually: an unknown field is not zero.
  • Only the search text or barcode is sent to Open Food Facts.
  • Offline, recently chosen products and manual entry are available.
  • If the service is overloaded or there are too many requests, wait a few seconds or minutes.

Open Food Facts data is licensed under ODbL 1.0.

Insulin and carbs on board

GlucoPilot uses simple research models. They do not measure insulin concentration and are not pharmacological profiles.

Rapid insulin (IOB)

  • The action profile depends on the group: rapid-acting analogs — 4 hours with a peak at about 1 hour; ultra-rapid (Fiasp, Lyumjev) — peak about 10 minutes earlier and slightly shorter action; regular human insulin (Actrapid, Humulin R) — about 7 hours with a peak after about 2 hours.
  • Active bolus — the part of a dose that has not acted yet.
  • Only recognised rapid products are counted (see “Diary”).

Tresiba and other basal insulins

  • They are not included in the active bolus.
  • The Tresiba indicator is a simplified linear overlap of doses over 42 hours. It is an illustration, not measured activity.

Carbohydrates (COB)

  • Carbohydrates are absorbed over at least 3 hours. Fat and protein extend absorption up to 6 hours.
  • For the charts the carbohydrate equivalent is carbs + 0.5 × protein + 0.1 × fat.
  • If protein or fat is unknown, a neutral scenario is used and this is marked.

ISF and ICR

  • ISF — how many mmol/L glucose falls per 1 unit of rapid insulin.
  • ICR in GlucoPilot — how many units of insulin correspond to 10 g of carbohydrates (U/10 g).

How they are estimated

  1. The actual CGM change over each 5 minutes is taken — only between real readings 3–7 minutes apart, with no invented values.
  2. Next to it, the modelled carbohydrate appearance and the modelled rapid-insulin action for the same step.
  3. For 6 points of the day (every 4 hours) it is estimated separately how much of the change is explained by food and how much by insulin. Nearby hours carry more weight.
  4. A smooth daily curve is drawn between the points; the table shows 3-hour averages.

The last 10 calendar days are used. Each node also estimates a separate background glucose drift (Tresiba, dawn phenomenon, liver) so that it does not distort ISF and ICR. Stabilisation is scaled to each input, so ISF is not pulled towards the starting value of 2.0. The Lab shows what share of each value comes from your data. The estimate counts as identified when there are at least 12 such intervals and they include both food and insulin.

Good to know

  • The first results need several days with CGM, food and rapid insulin.
  • If food and insulin always come together, their effects are hard to separate. Numbers are then hidden or marked as a hypothesis.
  • “Hypothesis” means the estimate has not yet been confirmed on independent days.
Do not change your therapy based on these numbers without your doctor.

Research lab

The Lab contains experimental analyses kept separate from the main forecast and the diary.

Controls

  • Choose a date with ◀ ▶. Now · current charts returns to today.
  • Whole day shows the full day. At a moment recreates the state at the chosen time without using later events.
  • Choose the time with the slider and tap Build for this time. Model tail length: +3, +6, +12 or +24 hours.

Charts

  1. CGM — actual readings and forecast up to +180 minutes; the orange mark is the chosen moment.
  2. Insulin action — NovoRapid, Tresiba and the remaining bolus action.
  3. Macro tails — modelled absorption of carbohydrates, protein and fat.
  4. Actual ΔG — the real CGM change over 5 minutes.
  5. Predicted ΔG — a comparison model with starting constants (ISF 2.0, ICR 10 g/U). These are not your coefficients.
  6. Two personal curves — ISF and ICR over the day, with an hourly table.
  7. Balance to target — deviation of the forecast from a nominal target of 6.0 mmol/L.
  8. Balance to target · ICR/ISF scenario — a separate calculation using your curves.

ISF + ICR hypothesis testing

The model is trained on older days and checked on later days that were not used for training. If food and insulin cannot be separated, numbers are hidden.

Theoretical equivalents in insulin units and grams are temporarily hidden in the balance charts for safety: such numbers are easy to mistake for a dose.

CGM analytics

CGM analytics

The 📊 CGM analytics button in Tools shows statistics for 7, 14, 30 or 90 days:

  • the share of readings in the ranges below 3.0; 3.0–3.8; 3.9–10.0; 10.1–13.9; above 13.9 mmol/L;
  • mean glucose and variability (CV);
  • coverage and the number of gaps longer than 10 minutes.

These are shares of received readings, not clinical time in range (TIR): gaps can distort the picture.

GMI

GMI (glucose management indicator) is calculated from mean glucose. Each 5-minute interval has equal weight; gaps are not filled in. You can log lab HbA1c results alongside for comparison.

Local analysis

  • Now — a description of the current situation from actual observations only.
  • Local research — repeating drops on the last complete days.
  • Autonomous Discovery — a search for repeating changes in 1–4-hour windows across the whole day. At least 5 previous days with CGM are needed.
  • Independent validation — a window is chosen on older days and checked on newer ones.

This is descriptive statistics of repetition, not a proven cause.

History import and backups

CGM history import

  • Export a CSV file (or a ZIP containing CSV) from xDrip+ and choose it via Tools → Import CGM history (xDrip+ CSV). Nightscout JSON (entries) is also supported.
  • The same is available in the Lab (Import xDrip+ history).
  • Duplicate timestamps are not added and existing records are not deleted. If the format is not recognised, the database is not changed.

Insulin import

Import insulin (xDrip+ JSON) transfers boluses from an xDrip+ or Nightscout treatments export.

Full archive (backup)

Tools → Backup and restore — one file with the whole database and settings.

  • Export full archive saves glucose, insulin, food, activity, the forecast log, ISF/ICR curve revisions, the HbA1c log and settings.
  • Restore from full archive (replace) first shows the date, version and number of records, then replaces all data on the device.
  • Current data is saved automatically before replacing. Undo last restore cancels the last restore.
  • To move to a new phone or tablet: export → transfer the file → install GlucoPilot → restore → restart the app.
The archive is not encrypted and contains medical data. Keep it in a safe place and never post it publicly.

The older JSON operations are hidden behind Show legacy JSON actions: they are only needed to move data from very old versions.

Common problems

No new readings

  • Is Broadcast locally on in xDrip+? Is Identify receiver empty or set to the correct package?
  • Is Receive CGM broadcasts checked in GlucoPilot?
  • Is xDrip+ itself receiving fresh data?
  • Are battery restrictions off for both apps?

Data stops while the screen is locked

Allow autostart and unrestricted background activity. Do not swipe the app away.

Forecast unavailable

A fresh CGM reading and at least 4 readings in the last 30 minutes without large gaps are needed. After a sensor gap, wait 20–30 minutes.

Insulin is not imported from xDrip+

Turn on xDrip Web Service in xDrip+. Import works only while GlucoPilot is open.

Insulin is not counted as active

Check the product name (see “Diary”) and the Basal tick.

No ISF/ICR

Several days of CGM, food and rapid insulin entries are needed. Unknown protein and fat reduce accuracy.

Product search does not work

Internet is required. If Open Food Facts is overloaded, try again later or enter the product manually.

Buttons are covered by system bars or the keyboard

Report the phone model, Android version and font size.

Privacy and security

  • Data is stored in a local database on the phone. There is no account and no cloud.
  • Only search text or barcodes are sent to Open Food Facts over the internet.
  • Insulin from xDrip+ is read through the phone’s own address (127.0.0.1); the data does not leave the device.
  • Glucose is accepted only from the xDrip+ BgEstimate broadcast. Another app on the phone could in theory send the same broadcast, so install only apps you trust.
  • Android backup is turned off: data does not go to a cloud automatically, but it is also deleted together with the app.
